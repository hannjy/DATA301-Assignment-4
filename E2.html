4. Does the variability appear constant over time? Explain what you see in the plot and whether a transformation might be appropriate.


For determining constant variability we want to look at the size of the fluctuations in number of beers produced. Looking back at the first plot showing the overall behavior of the series, when beer production was relatively low through 1950 to ~1975 the fluctuations are smaller and more constant in size. When the production reached its peak and averaged around 500 beers through the 1980’s to 1995 we can see greater fluctuations around the trend become bigger and irregular. In the more recent years when the number of beers produced decreases, the size of the fluctuations appear to decrease slightly as-well.

8. Would you detrend or transform the series before modelling it? Give a visual argument for your answer. Do not fit a model


I would consider de-trending the series to improve its stationarity. The time series shows an increasing and decreasing long-term trend in beer production which tells us the mean level of the series changes over time. The variance also does not remain constant over time, with specific periods having greater fluctuation than others. De-trending the series before modelling could help improve the accuracy and quality of results because the mean and variance wont be impacted by long-term trends.

#par(mfrow = c(2,1))
plot(ausbeer, main = "Beer Production with no Transformation")

plot(log(ausbeer), main = "Beer Production with Log Transformation")

Another method to improve the variance would be to transform the series with a log transformation. This stabilizes the variance by compressing larger values more than smaller ones, reducing the heteroscedasticity of the data. While we can see in the visualization that the previously noted areas of greater variance (~1990’s) have been almost “smushed down”, the general shape of the data has not changed significantly. This could be because the existing variance of the data is not scaled proportionally with the mean so applying the log transformation does not greatly improve the variance.

Since de-trending and transforming can both be applied to a time series I would recommend de-trending the series first to see if that improves the shape of the mean and variance, then transform with a log transformation if it would add some meaningful benefit when going on to model the data.
